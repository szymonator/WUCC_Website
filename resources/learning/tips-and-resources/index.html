Climbing Tips & Resources

"Hi all, here is a collection of tips and resources I’ve found useful to my climbing and wish I’d known when I’d starting climbing. Hope you find it useful." — Sid, September 2017

Skin Care & Chalk
Skin Care
  • Your hands regrow skin most quickly when moisturised (but don’t moisturise in the hours immediately before climbing; though some disagree).
  • After Training:
    1. Sand down callouses until skin is smooth (use sandpaper/pumice stone)
    2. Small amount of sanding on fingertips.
    3. Wash all chalk off hands.
    4. Apply ClimbSkin (ClimbOn is better for really raw skin)
  • Use finger tape to cover up skin injuries while climbing (until no longer raw).
  • Use nail scissors/trimmers to remove excess bits of skin.
  • Sharp nail clippers are really good for cutting off flappers - wash it, cut, then apply a plaster. Keep one on for at least 2 days and use climbing tape. Applying some sort of ointment can help but personally mine have healed perfectly without it.
  • Wear gloves when doing the washing up!
  • If you need to recover skin quickly take baths instead of showers, keeping your hands dry (or do what the pros do and wear gloves in the shower).
  • Split Tips Guide
  • For taping up flappers/split tips Kevin Jorgeson recommends Elastoplast Fabric Tape, I reckon 3M Friction Tape is better.
  • BMC Skincare Guide (Video)
  • Antihydral (at own risk)
  • Other creams which climbers use: Elizabeth Arden 8 Hour Cream Original / Bag Balm
Chalk
  • Decathlon chalk is awful, don’t buy it.
  • Personally I mix chunky decathlon chalk with powdery Seven Sisters chalk for a nice combo. The only reason I use it is because it was a birthday gift and I’m not about to waste free chalk.
  • FrictionLabs Bam Bam Chalk - Chunky is the absolute worst I ever used though. I don’t care if it’s “the best”, it's so bad.
  • Liquid chalk can make a big difference when you have thin fingertip skin.
  • People with mega sweaty hands may want to consider using liquid chalk regularly.
  • Magdust is insanely good but obviously it’s expensive, treat it like gold dust and save it for memorable trips!
  • BRUSH YOUR CLIMBS! Not only is it good etiquette but it’s an insane improvement for grip
Shoe Sizing & Purchasing Gear
Shoe Sizing
  • Rock + Run Sizing Guide / Bananafingers Shoeculator
  • How to choose climbing shoes.
  • Cut your toenails before trying on shoes.
Purchasing Gear
  • Go Outdoors have a price matching policy where if you can find one of their products on another site/in a different store for a cheaper price then not only will they match it but they will knock 10% off it. They have a store in Canley.
  • UKClimbing.com’s For Sale/Wanted Forum is good for finding second-hand bargains.
  • The Facebook group ‘Outdoor Gear Exchange’ is also good.
  • Be careful buying second-hand ‘soft gear’ like ropes, slings etc.
  • AlpineTrek.co.uk often has great deals, especially during the summer!

Injury Prevention Exercises
  • Always warm up before climbing. Get your heartrate up then do some easy climbs.
  • Rest between climbs.
  • Do not even look at a fingerboard or campus board until you have been climbing for years.

Staying Warm in Winter
Staying Warm in Winter
  • Wear all your layers while walking to the crag, so you’re overheating when you arrive.
  • Down Jackets, Thermals (Merino Wool/Synthetic Base Layer), Gloves, Hats
  • Wear lots of layers.
  • Take a hot drink.
  • Put your shoes inside your jacket when not climbing.
  • Stick hand warmers in your pockets / hands under your armpits.
  • Consider buying belay gloves, they’re not insulated but they do make a difference.
